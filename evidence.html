Evidence and safety by design

Which feature answers which problem, and what the research does (and does not) say. Sources were checked online in November 2026. Where the evidence is thin we say so.

A. Feature-to-evidence table

FeatureRoot cause it targetsEvidenceStrength and limits
Overnight lockout and emergency whitelistLate-night phone use shortens and delays sleep, which hurts mood and study.Hale & Guan (2015) reviewed 67 studies: screen time was linked to worse sleep, mainly shorter and later sleep, in about 90% of them (PMC4437561). Student studies agree: bedtime device use and night-time phone checking predict poor sleep (Vietnam, Saudi Arabia).Moderate Associations, not proof of cause. Mostly self-reported. Our lockout only governs MindAnchor itself.
Time caps and Pause before you scrollAutomatic scroll loops and weak in-the-moment self-control.Design basis: choice architecture and commitment, as described by Thaler & Sunstein, Nudge (Yale University Press, 2008). Procrastination and impulsiveness are consistent predictors of self-regulation failure (Steel, 2007, below).Conceptual We did not find a study testing this exact feature, so treat it as a design rationale, not proven benefit.
AI Micro-Task Slicer and 15-minute focus sprintsAcademic avoidance: tasks that feel aversive and too big.Steel (2007) meta-analysis: task aversiveness, low self-efficacy and impulsiveness are strong, consistent predictors of procrastination (paper). Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), 94 tests: specific "when and what" plans improved goal achievement (d = 0.65) (paper).Moderate A later 2024 synthesis found smaller effects after bias correction (d about 0.15 to 0.35). The slicer is rule-based, not a live AI.
Guided decompression player (ambient, Punjabi rhythms, own songs, Spotify)Study stress and fatigue, and the urge to switch off with short-form video.de Witte et al. (2020): 104 randomised trials, 9,617 people. Music interventions reduced stress, with small-to-moderate effects (PubMed).Moderate The review covers music interventions in general. We found no study on Punjabi playlists specifically.
Breathing reset (box, long exhale) and 5-4-3-2-1 groundingAcute stress and physical arousal.Balban et al. (2023), a randomised trial: five minutes a day of structured breathing, especially cyclic sighing with long exhales, improved mood more than mindfulness meditation (PubMed). Box breathing was one of the tested methods.Promising One trial, authors call it "promising". Our long-exhale option is similar to, not identical with, cyclic sighing. The grounding exercise has no citation here.
Get Real Help button, local helplines, Quick ExitIsolation, stigma and friction when in distress.Gould et al. (2025): 437 suicidal callers to the US Lifeline were interviewed about two weeks later; nearly all said the call helped (PMC12099483).Supportive Caller self-reports from the US, not a controlled trial and not about Pakistan. Quick Exit is a safety design choice with no study cited.
Mood check-in with a gentle support promptLow self-awareness, and a high barrier to asking for help.No study cited. This is a design rationale: a private, one-tap check-in that never scores or shames, and offers help after two low days.No evidence claimed
Skippable 20-second walkthroughConfusion when first opening an app.Nielsen Norman Group found app tutorials often do not improve task performance and recommends usable design instead (article).Honest trade-off Because of this, our tour is short, skippable and can be replayed, and the screens are meant to be self-explanatory.

B. Coverage of the problems (not just screen time)

ProblemHow MindAnchor responds
Digital overuse and doomscrollingTime caps with a chosen app list, 20-second pause before scrolling, "time saved" feedback.
Sleep loss and fatigueOvernight lockout with an emergency whitelist, sleep timer and sleep sounds.
Academic avoidance and burnoutTask slicer into four 15-minute steps, focus sprints, progress bars.
Stress and overloadAmbient and Punjabi sound, own songs and Spotify, breathing, grounding.
Isolation and acute crisisAlways-visible Get Real Help, Pakistan, US and personal helplines, supportive prompt after low check-ins.

C. Safety by design

RequirementWhat the app does
No identifiable mental-health data without consentNickname only, no email, no real name. All data is in the browser. Mood check-ins are saved only after an explicit yes, can be turned off and deleted in Me, and the whole profile can be wiped. A downloadable copy is available.
No monitoring by staff or parentsNo server, no analytics, no admin view, no sharing feature. Only the person using the device can see anything.
No shamingNo streaks, no red warnings, no missed-goal penalties. Messages celebrate "time saved" and small steps. A reached cap says "great boundary".
A clear path to a real personGet Real Help is on every screen. It shows local helplines, and Quick Exit (button or ESC) leaves instantly.

D. Honest limits

  • MindAnchor is a prototype web app. It cannot read or block other apps on a phone. Real enforcement would need a native Android app with usage-access permission.
  • Accounts exist only in one browser. Clearing browser data or switching devices loses them, and passwords cannot be reset.
  • Dashboard figures are sample data until the user has real activity.
  • Spotify plays in Spotify's own player. Full tracks need the listener to be logged in to Spotify.
  • Helpline numbers and hours can change. Confirm them with your university counselling service before relying on them.
  • This app supports wellbeing. It is not therapy or medical advice.
