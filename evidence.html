Evidence and safety by design

Every feature is traced to a ranked problem, a root cause from our 5 Whys, our own interview and survey data (8 interviews P1 to P8, 31 male student survey responses), and published research. Published sources were checked online; where evidence is indirect or missing, we say so.

Our ranked problems: #1 short-form video doomscroll loop · #2 academic avoidance and procrastination · #3 late-night sleep loss · #4 counsellor hesitancy and stigma · #5 social comparison and academic FOMO · #6 physical co-isolation ("alone together").
Root causes (5 Whys): an automatic phone reflex at night, infinite-scroll feeds with variable rewards, depleted self-control after long study hours, and no friction boundary between study and frictionless entertainment.

C. Safety by design

RequirementWhat the app does
No identifiable mental-health data without consentNickname only, no email or real name. All data stays in the browser. Mood check-ins are saved only after an explicit yes, can be turned off and deleted in Me, and the whole profile can be wiped. A copy can be downloaded.
No monitoring by staff or parentsNo server, analytics, admin view or sharing feature. Our own survey says 22.6% would uninstall an app that shared data, and Idea 1 (parent-gated access) was cut for this reason.
No shamingNo streaks, red warnings or penalties. "Time saved" and small wins. A reached cap says "great boundary".
A clear path to a real personGet Real Help on every screen with Pakistan helplines, Quick Exit (button or ESC), and Rest-mode access to help.

D. Honest limits

  • Prototype web app. It cannot read or block other apps on a phone. Real enforcement needs a native Android app.
  • Anonymous peer circles (Idea 10) were deferred: they need a server and moderation, which would break our local-only privacy rule and create safety risks. This is a deliberate cut, not an oversight.
  • Our evidence comes from 8 interviews (convenience sample, home settings) and 31 survey responses, mostly first-year male students living at home. It cannot be generalised.
  • Screen time was self-reported. Research finds self-reports rarely match logged use (Parry et al., 2021).
  • Accounts exist only in one browser, and passwords cannot be reset. Dashboard figures are sample data until real use.
  • Helpline numbers and hours change. Confirm them with the university counselling service.
  • This app supports wellbeing and is not therapy or medical advice.
